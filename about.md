I live in Accra, Ghana. Most of my day is PHP, Python and TypeScript, with Ruby for the things I build for myself. Laravel is where I'm most at home — I've shipped enough with it to know both why people love it and where it will bite you.

This site is where I write down the things that cost me a day to figure out: an API that needed better documentation, a permissions package that didn't expect UUID primary keys, a deploy that only failed in production. If a post saves you that day, it did its job.

### Work

I'm CTO at [Mizormor](https://mizormor.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, a trip planning and tourism business management platform for travellers touring Africa. I lead engineering: the architecture, the APIs the apps run on, and the calls about what we build ourselves and what we buy.

Before Mizormor I was lead software engineer at [Kwarleyz Group](https://kwarleyzgroup.com){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}, where I led the team that built [JobsByForce](https://jobsbyforce.ai){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} — a hiring platform built for African talent markets.

JobsByForce matches candidates to roles on embeddings rather than keyword search, and crawls listings across Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa, Egypt and Algeria. There is an AI assistant on both sides of the hire: one that walks a candidate through applying, and a multi-agent system that takes an employer from writing the job post to grading the screening answers. Applications carry their own state machine, escrow and payments included.

It runs on Supabase and Deno edge functions, with a Next.js front end that also ships as a mobile build. Most of the interesting problems were in the matching — deciding what a good match actually is, in a market where job titles mean different things in different countries.

### Open source

### Community
